The heart behind the journeys

Some encounters
stay with you.

Two personal stories from Brent’s time in Athens. Each began with an ordinary evening walk. Take a moment to step inside.

01Athens · From Brent’s bookThe red juice boxAn encounter I thought I understood.Read the story +

An abridged account from Chapter 1 · Brent Kern

The evening settled slowly over Athens. Heat still rose from the pavement. Between two apartment buildings, the Acropolis appeared again, then slipped behind the city.

There were twelve of us walking that night. I shifted a canvas bag from one hand to the other. Inside were sandwiches, snacks, and juice boxes. Every few blocks, someone stopped to talk. Relationships set the rhythm.

A moment on the street

When I looked up, a woman was standing directly in front of me. She was already speaking, her hands moving as much as her voice. We didn’t understand each other.

I handed her a sandwich, a snack, and a juice box. She accepted the food. Then she shook her head and pushed the drink back. She pointed inside the bag.

Artur came back, listened, and said quietly, “She wants a red juice box.”

I looked again. Blue, blue, nothing else.

Finally, she took only the sandwich and walked away. I stood there with the drink in my hand. Honestly, I didn’t know what else I could have done. A quiet old thought slipped in.

Sometimes you just get what you get…

We kept walking. The streets kept introducing us to people. By the time we headed back to the apartment, that encounter had slipped into the background.

Back at the apartment

Stories started to move around the room. Who did you meet? What was it like?

“You all are not gonna believe what happened to me tonight,” I said. I told them about the woman and the drink I couldn’t give her. I thought I was about to tell the strangest story in the room.

Amanda had been listening.

“Brent… she wanted a red juice box.”

I gave a little laugh. Of course I knew that. Amanda didn’t move on. Same gentle voice, same steady eyes. She said my name and stopped.

In that pause, something began to happen inside me. I had always thought compassion was mainly about doing, serving, helping, fixing. Suddenly I saw that before any of that, there’s just noticing. Actually seeing a person.

I hadn’t done that. I had turned a person into a need to meet, and missed her own preferences. Amanda’s voice brought me back.

“She just wanted a red juice box.”

02Athens · From Brent’s bookAn evening with JohnA park bench. A conversation. A reason to return.Read the story +

An abridged account from Chapter 2 · Brent Kern

We followed Artur into the park as the evening light began to thin. At one bench, he introduced me to John, a man from England who had lived in Greece for about thirty years.

Artur offered to finish the walk with the team and return in twenty minutes. Would I like to sit with John?

“That would be perfect. I’d love that.”

A little time together

We talked about ordinary things. A little about himself, a little about England. There was no great unfolding of his life that evening. It was simply the sort of conversation two people have when they have just been introduced and find that they enjoy talking.

I enjoyed talking with John.

The next night wasn’t my turn to go on the walk. I asked to go anyway. With Artur’s agreement, I went ahead to the park.

John was there. He was surprised that I had come back. Pleased, too.

I sat down again.

The bench he called his office

We had about an hour this time, though I wasn’t keeping track of it that way. The bench was his office. That was what he called it. He usually had a novel to read.

Little by little, he told me more. Leaving England. Building a life in Greece. Marriage and divorce. The routines and arrangements that helped him get through each day.

Then he told me about his daughter and granddaughter. His daughter wanted nothing to do with him, and he wasn’t part of their lives. That hurt him.

I couldn’t fill in everything that had happened. I was hearing John’s part of it, sitting beside him while he told me what it was like to be outside those relationships. I stayed and listened.

When it was time to go

The team returned. I reached out and shook his hand. I told him I would try to visit again, though I couldn’t promise when.

Until I saw him again, I said, take care of yourself.

He looked at me.

“Brent, the problem isn’t taking care of myself.”
“The problem is loneliness.”

Stay with the moment

Perhaps there is someone whose story you have only begun to hear.
