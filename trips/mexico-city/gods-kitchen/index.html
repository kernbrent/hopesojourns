01 · The purpose

Restore dignity.
Offer hope.

God’s Kitchen connects with people experiencing homelessness in Mexico City through food, practical care, friendship, prayer, and encouragement.

Its goal is immediate nutritional relief alongside a structured path toward support and recovery. A meal is a way to begin a relationship, listen to a person’s story, and respond to the needs they choose to share.

2,878

people experiencing homelessness are cited in the ministry’s briefing, referencing Mexico City’s Secretariat of Welfare and Social Equality.

The briefing does not specify the survey date; this is the context figure supplied by the ministry.

The reality behind the work

People living
without a home.

In the city center and other areas, people find shelter in parks, storm drains, and public spaces. The briefing describes housing insecurity alongside migration, unemployment, unstable support networks, physical and mental health needs, substance dependence, exploitation, and street-level drug activity.

The local team seeks to know the person beyond those circumstances. It works among people experiencing long-term homelessness, displaced young people, and street-connected communities, including areas of Cuauhtémoc and Venustiano Carranza.

02 · The work

What are
we doing?

Start with simple acts of care. Build trust through relationships. Offer a next step when a person is ready.

01

Share a hot meal

Weekly distributions of nourishing food address an immediate need and create time for personal connection.

02

Restore dignity

Clothing and personal hygiene kits provide practical care, offered with respect for each person.

03

Instill hope

Consistent, dignified human contact encourages a new beginning without overwhelming the person.

04

Offer pathways to support

Connect people who choose assistance with a partner rehabilitation center, recovery home, shelter, support program, hospital, or church.

03 · An outreach day

Practical care.
Thoughtful preparation.

  1. 01

    Prepare the food

    Prepare soup with chicken or beef broth, tomato, and vegetables or beans. Serve it with crackers or tortillas and a flavored water drink.

  2. 02

    Load and travel

    Pack thermal containers, folding tables, eco-friendly disposables, and clean water into the ministry vehicle.

  3. 03

    Meet people

    Set up an accessible station in a designated, well-lit public square or near a transport hub. Share food, listen, and offer practical care.

  4. 04

    Clean up and reflect

    Pack the equipment, leave the public area clean, and return to base for a volunteer debrief.

04 · Consistent presence

A meal today.
A relationship over time.

The ministry’s plan of action rests on three connected commitments.

Immediate relief

A hearty, nutritious meal each week, together with clothing and hygiene supplies.

Hope for new beginnings

Return to the same place and time, week after week. Consistency gives trust the time it needs.

A voluntary next step

Through specialized “bridge volunteers,” gradually introduce available support and accompany people who choose to accept assistance.

The invitation

Food.
Friendship.
Restoration.

God’s Kitchen expresses its Christian purpose through feeding people, building friendships, praying, encouraging, and reaching out to those who are hurting.

Support helps the ministry feed people who are hungry, provide clothing where it is needed, and build relationships and pathways toward restoration.

05 · The vision

What we
hope to achieve.

The briefing describes a long-term commitment to the person. These are ministry goals; each person’s path and choices are individual.

  1. 01

    In six months

    Build trust and an individual relationship through consistency.

  2. 02

    In twelve months

    Invite the person to consider available assistance and help connect them with the appropriate support.

  3. 03

    Over the longer term

    Work toward restoration, reintegration, and a life beyond the streets.
