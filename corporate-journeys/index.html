Heart transformation and leadership

See the person. Examine your heart. Change your practice.

An encounter may challenge the distance between the values we profess and the decisions we make. The work continues when leaders bring that awareness into everyday relationships.

With staff

Make it safer to speak.

Listen without preparing a defense. Invite disagreement, respond fairly to mistakes, and make commitments you can keep. Examine whose voice is missing when decisions affect people’s work.

With clients

Put integrity into service.

Look beyond the transaction. Ask better questions, communicate limitations honestly, and take responsibility when the organization falls short. Treat a client’s vulnerability with care.

With peers

Share responsibility and credit.

Bring curiosity into conflict. Surface tradeoffs, honor colleagues’ contributions, and address concerns directly. Work across functions with a shared responsibility for the people affected.

Led with integrity

Respect for people shapes every part of the journey.

Local partners identify useful work, guide service, and help evaluate the experience. Their communities have knowledge, gifts, and leadership to contribute. Participants arrive as guests who can offer help and also receive insight.

People decide whether to share their stories, participate in a conversation, or be photographed. We never ask someone to perform hardship or personal transformation for a visiting group. Assistance does not depend on sharing a story, a photograph, or a belief.

What integrity looks like

  • Projects shaped by partner priorities, available capacity, and appropriate participant skills.
  • Clear expectations for conduct, boundaries, consent, and respectful storytelling.
  • Facilitated reflection that examines leaders’ own choices and assumptions.
  • Honest planning about time, costs, responsibilities, and what a short visit can accomplish.
  • Follow-through that respects partner feedback and makes room to change course.

Before, during, and after

A journey with a path back to work.

We shape the experience through a planning conversation with your organization and local hosts. Dates, destination, service activities, facilitation, and costs are confirmed together.

  1. 01 / Prepare

    Name the leadership challenge.

    Identify who is coming, what needs to change at work, and how the team will listen and serve responsibly. Establish a personal behavior baseline and agree on privacy and participation expectations.

  2. 02 / Serve and reflect

    Let encounters ask difficult questions.

    Contribute to useful work under local leadership. Take time to notice assumptions, hear stories freely shared, and consider what compassion and integrity require of you.

  3. 03 / Return and practice

    Make the change visible.

    Choose specific actions for staff, clients, and peers. Use the curriculum’s 30-, 60-, and 90-day review plan to seek feedback, examine decisions, and adjust your practice.

The curriculum

Leadership Transformation
